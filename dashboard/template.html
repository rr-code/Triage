Comparison

Three versions of the same system ran over the identical set of payments, so the difference between them is a fair measurement, not a coincidence of luck.

The three configurations
Unguarded baseline
Retries every failed payment on a fixed schedule. No safety checks, no understanding of why any payment failed. This is what a lot of merchants run today.
Baseline + guardrails
The exact same blind retry logic, but every action is checked against a set of hard safety rules before it's allowed to happen — for example, a card that's permanently dead never gets retried.
Triage (full system)
Reads the decline reason and the case's history before deciding what to do, and is still checked by the same safety rules as the other two.

Decline codes

Every failed payment comes back from the bank with a reason code. The code tells Triage whether trying again is worth it at all, and if so, on which payment method.

Soft, hard, and ambiguous
Soft
A temporary problem — for example, not enough money in the account right now. Worth retrying once there's been time for the problem to resolve on its own.
Hard
The payment method itself is dead — an expired card, a closed account, a mandate the customer cancelled. Retrying the same card recovers nothing. The only way forward is a different payment method.
Ambiguous
The bank gave a vague "do not honour" response that could mean almost anything. Worth one careful retry, but repeating it over and over wastes effort without learning anything new.

How it works

Every payment moves through five stages. The fourth, the gate, is the one thing no strategy is ever allowed to skip.

Detect
Filters raw events down to cases actually worth pursuing, and ranks them.
Decide
A strategy proposes one action for the case, with a reason.
Gate
Checks the proposal against hard safety rules. Blocks it if it fails any of them.
Act
The only stage allowed to touch the outside world — the payment gateway, a message.
Measure
Rolls every case's outcome up into the numbers on this page.

Why the gate is its own separate step: if safety checks lived inside each strategy, a new or careless strategy could simply forget to include them — or a future AI-driven strategy could reason its way around them. Making the gate a separate stage that every proposal must pass through, regardless of which strategy produced it, means no strategy can opt out of the safety rules even by accident.
